定位：公开数据的事件重建、全通道分布、测量敏感性和完整模型族审计。所有图均为二次分析证据，不构成刺激疗效或前瞻性临床验证。

## Supplementary Figure S1. Reconstruction and attrition of visual-stimulation analysis windows

**Legend.** (A) Representative reconstruction of the 10-Hz block in participant sub-001. Vertical grey ticks show hardware photic pulses. The frequency label preceded the annotated eye opening by 1.611 s. The annotated eyes-open interval extended from 1.611 to 5.555 s after the label; the strict signal-analysis interval excluded the first 0.5 s after eye opening and the final 0.1 s before eye closing. (B) Reconstructed eyes-open duration for all usable 5-, 10-, 15-, and 20-Hz blocks. Boxes show the median and interquartile range; points are blocks, not independent participants. (C) Delay from the frequency label to the annotated eye opening. (D) Block-level flow from 407 nominal frequency-labelled blocks to 372 reconstructable eyes-open blocks and 351 blocks at the four main frequencies meeting the strict signal-analysis duration criterion. The final step therefore combines the prespecified 5/10/15/20-Hz restriction with the duration criterion. The 351 blocks yielded 311 participant-by-frequency summaries; participant-level inference did not treat blocks as independent observations.

**Evidence boundary.** The figure validates event reconstruction and effective exposure. It does not establish that eye state alone caused differences between the eyes-open and closed-eye analyses because region, window and response definition also differed.

**Audit sources.** `data/public/ds006036/v1.0.6/sub-*/eeg/*_events.tsv`; `outputs/qc/ds006036_full_foundation/occipital_block_metrics.csv`; derived tables `FigureS1_event_blocks.csv`, `FigureS1_flow.csv`, and `FigureS1_representative_timeline.csv`.

## Supplementary Figure S2. Scalp-wide distributions of visual and auditory frequency-following responses

**Legend.** (A) Median channel-level local signal-to-noise ratio during eyes-open visual stimulation at 5, 10, 15, and 20 Hz. O1 and O2 produced the largest or joint-largest median response at every tested frequency. Cell values are descriptive channel medians; the heatmap is not a source-localization analysis. (B) Channel-level median and interquartile range of the 40-Hz auditory stimulation-minus-rest response across 35 participants. Positive responses were broadly distributed over the low-density scalp montage. The visual and auditory panels use different response contrasts and must not be compared as a common amplitude scale.

**Evidence boundary.** These sensor-level distributions support montage/ROI justification. They do not localize cortical generators or demonstrate directed propagation.

**Audit sources.** `outputs/qc/ds006036_full_foundation/channel_frequency_summary.csv`; `outputs/qc/ds005048_full_foundation/channel_response_summary.csv`; derived tables `FigureS2_visual_channel_summary.csv` and `FigureS2_auditory_channel_summary.csv`.

## Supplementary Figure S3. Response-scale, reference and influence sensitivity

**Legend.** (A) Adjusted AD-minus-control and FTD-minus-control 10-Hz driving-index contrasts with heteroskedasticity-consistent 95% confidence intervals on the raw, log1p-transformed and 5th/95th-percentile-winsorized scales. Each scale has its own numerical axis. Direction was consistent, whereas whether the interval excluded zero depended on the response scale. (B) Spearman rank consistency of visual posterior–frontal absolute imaginary coherence and debiased weighted phase-lag index across average, native and current-source-density reference schemes at four frequencies. (C) Corresponding reference consistency for the auditory central–frontal measures. (D) Full-sample MMSE coefficients and leave-one-subject-out ranges for the auditory central-minus-frontal SNR gradient and current-source-density absolute imaginary coherence. Both influence ranges crossed zero and changed sign.

**Evidence boundary.** Reference correlations evaluate participant ranking under alternative transforms, not agreement of raw numerical values. Leave-one-out instability argues against a robust individual clinical association but does not prove exact absence of an effect.

**Audit sources.** `outputs/competition/ds006036_competitor_models/direct_10hz_models.csv`; visual and auditory `reference_sensitivity.csv`; `outputs/network/ds005048_models/leave_one_out_stability.csv`; derived `FigureS3_*.csv` files.

## Supplementary Figure S4. Complete advanced-model family gate after multiplicity control

**Legend.** (A) Minimum Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted q value within each frozen model family; *m* denotes the number of models in that family. No family crossed q=0.05. The full auditory exploratory family had a minimum q of 0.883, whereas the subset testing incremental value beyond local SNR had a minimum q of 0.995. (B) Adjusted q values for the 16 visual diagnosis models across four frequencies, two primary outcomes and two exploratory outcomes. (C) Adjusted q values for the corresponding 16 patient-severity models. (D) Adjusted q values for 32 models relating four resting EEG features to two advanced visual measures at four frequencies. The smallest q in panel D was 0.110.

**Evidence boundary.** The panels show failure to pass the prespecified multiplicity gates. They do not establish equivalence to zero. The auditory full-family and incremental-subset minima are deliberately reported separately and must not be interchanged.

**Audit sources.** `outputs/network/ds006036_models/diagnosis_group_models.csv`, `patient_severity_models.csv`, and `rest_to_advanced_visual_models.csv`; `outputs/network/ds005048_models/mmse_models.csv`; derived `FigureS4_*.csv` files.
