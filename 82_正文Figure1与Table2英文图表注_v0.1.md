## Figure 1

**Figure 1. Seven public human EEG cohorts test four transitions from an observed result to a stronger claim.** **A**, Cohort-by-transition role matrix. A direct role indicates that the cohort contributed an estimate designed to test the corresponding transition; context indicates a reference phenotype, external mechanism-specificity constraint, or data-availability boundary. Blank cells indicate no direct role. These labels do not encode effect direction, evidence strength, or a meta-analytic weight. **B**, Antecedent evidence, stronger claim, direct evidence, and decision for each transition. T1 asks whether a detectable or diagnostically sensitive frequency-following response establishes a state-general endogenous mechanism. T2 asks whether high within-recording reliability establishes metric- and aggregation-level-matched cognitive validity. T3 asks whether a mean behavioral advantage over an active rhythmic comparator establishes within-participant neural–behavioral coupling. T4 asks whether an in-sample neural association provides incremental prediction in held-out participants. Positive findings at the antecedent level are retained when the stronger claim is not established. Evidence is juxtaposed across cohorts; participants and raw EEG amplitudes are not pooled across modalities or datasets. The analyses are secondary analyses of public human data and do not test chronic treatment efficacy or disease modification.

## Table 2

**Table 2. Four inferential transitions from rhythmic-sensory EEG findings to stronger mechanistic, cognitive, or predictive claims.**

**Note.** Each row preserves the antecedent finding and evaluates the proposed stronger claim using its own estimand. `Not established` and `Not established in tested settings` indicate that the available result was conditional, imprecise, or lacked a mechanism-discriminating comparison; neither denotes a universal null effect. `Not supported for tested coupling estimand` is restricted to the reported within-participant neural–behavioral coefficient. `No consistent predictive increment` refers to the reported participant-held-out or nested analyses and does not negate an in-sample association. Effective sample sizes are outcome specific and are not additive across rows. Within-recording split-half reliability is not between-day test–retest reliability. CI, confidence interval; FSIQ, full-scale intelligence quotient; ITPC, inter-trial phase coherence; MMSE, Mini-Mental State Examination; RT, response time; SNR, signal-to-noise ratio.
