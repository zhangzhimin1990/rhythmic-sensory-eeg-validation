# Alzheimer's & Dementia submission adaptation package v1

## Title

Testing cognitive validity of rhythmic-sensory EEG target engagement

## Structured abstract

**INTRODUCTION:** Rhythmic sensory stimulation elicits stimulus-locked electroencephalographic responses, but whether these responses qualify as cognitive or clinical markers remains uncertain. We tested four transitions from target engagement to stronger inference.

**METHODS:** We analyzed seven public human EEG datasets spanning cognitive impairment, aging, and development. We separated detectability, robustness, within-recording reliability, cognitive validity, neural–behavioral coupling, and held-out prediction. Raw EEG from 35 older adults was analyzed under a condition-blind frozen protocol.

**RESULTS:** Closed-eye 10-Hz selectivity differed by diagnosis but had low split-half reliability and imprecise within-diagnosis cognitive associations. In older adults, the 40-Hz auditory response was stable (0.838), whereas its Mini-Mental State Examination association was 0.085 (95% CI, −0.283 to 0.417). In the frozen cohort, personalized theta versus non-rhythmic stimulation increased frontocentral inter-trial phase coherence by 0.088 (95% CI, 0.051 to 0.124) and evoked local signal-to-noise ratio by 7.52 dB (95% CI, 4.52 to 10.51). Median response time decreased by 27.73 ms (95% CI, −36.17 to −19.28), but condition-specific neural–behavioral coupling and consistent incremental prediction were unsupported.

**DISCUSSION:** Rhythmic-sensory EEG responses supported target engagement and an acute performance advantage, but not a condition-specific cognitive mechanism proxy. Reliability, group sensitivity, cognitive validity, coupling, and prediction require separate qualification before an EEG response can support biomarker, treatment-selection, or surrogate-endpoint claims.

**TRIAL REGISTRATION:** Not applicable; no participants were enrolled or treated.

## Research in Context

**1. Systematic review:** We searched PubMed, bibliographic databases, trial registries, and public-data records for human rhythmic visual, auditory, and audiovisual stimulation studies reporting EEG, cognition, reliability, or prediction. Prior work showed robust stimulus-frequency responses but heterogeneous mechanistic and cognitive findings.

**2. Interpretation:** Across seven public cohorts, we found that target engagement, within-recording reliability, diagnostic sensitivity, cognitive validity, neural–behavioral coupling, and held-out prediction were not interchangeable. Frozen raw-EEG confirmation showed both target engagement and an acute performance advantage without validating the measured phase response as their condition-specific behavioral mechanism.

**3. Future directions:** Prospective patient studies should prespecify state, metric, active comparator, between-day reliability, within-participant clinical coupling, and external prediction before qualifying rhythmic-sensory EEG as a treatment-selection marker or surrogate endpoint.

## Highlights

- Seven public EEG cohorts tested four inferential transitions.
- Reliable frequency-following responses lacked consistent cognitive validity.
- Personalized theta improved target engagement and acute task performance.
- Neural–behavioral coupling and held-out prediction were unsupported.
- Target engagement alone cannot qualify a cognitive or surrogate marker.

## Abbreviated summary

Across seven public human EEG cohorts, rhythmic-sensory responses were often detectable and reliable, and personalized theta improved acute performance. However, diagnostic sensitivity, cognitive validity, condition-specific neural–behavioral coupling, and held-out prediction separated, limiting biomarker and surrogate-endpoint claims.

## Mandatory scope statement for the cover letter

This work does not test chronic treatment efficacy or disease modification. Its AD/ADRD contribution is to define, with original human EEG analyses, the evidence required before a rhythmic-sensory response can be interpreted as a cognitive biomarker, treatment-selection marker, or surrogate endpoint. The frozen same-cohort confirmation preserves the positive findings of physiological target engagement and acute behavioral advantage while showing that the measured phase response was not validated as their condition-specific behavioral mechanism.

## Disease-heterogeneity paragraph for manuscript integration

The cohorts do not represent a single disease continuum. AD and FTD share cognitive impairment and resting spectral slowing but differ in pathology, topography, and clinical phenotype; cognitively normal older adults additionally differ from patients in both disease burden and available cognitive range. We therefore did not pool participant-level effects or treat diagnosis as an exchangeable covariate across cohorts. Disease-related analyses were confined to linked AD, FTD, and control recordings, whereas independent healthy-aging and developmental cohorts tested measurement properties and inferential transitions. This design addresses heterogeneity by limiting each estimate to the population, state, modality, and outcome that generated it, while using cross-cohort convergence only to evaluate the logic of claim qualification.
