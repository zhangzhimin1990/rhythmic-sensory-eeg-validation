## Figure 2

**Figure 2. Outcome-specific sample flow and public-data boundaries.** Counts are shown separately for the public release, signal or structural gate, primary estimation sample, and outcome-specific sample. The units differ across cells and include participants, records, runs, blocks, trials, and participant–condition rows; counts must therefore not be added across columns. The ds006222 release passed a six-record signal sample gate, but the prespecified behavioral and occlusion analyses could not be reconstructed from the public files. The two neural exclusions in ds007648 arose from frame misalignment and recording truncation. For ds006780, the figure separates released ASSR data, technical eligibility, d-prime availability, and FSIQ availability. For the personalized-theta dataset, the released table contains all 44 participants; the raw-BDF R2 technical gate passed in three of five development participants, and the amended condition-blind R2b gate subsequently passed in 35 of 39 untouched validation participants. Exclusions and unavailable outcomes are data-quality or availability boundaries, not exclusions based on neural response magnitude or behavioral benefit.

## Figure 3

**Figure 3. Response detectability, within-recording reliability, and population sensitivity are distinct measurement properties.** **A**, Proportions of participants with a positive local frequency-following response at 5, 10, 15, and 20 Hz in ds006036 and a positive stimulation-minus-rest 40-Hz response in ds005048. Error bars are Wilson 95% confidence intervals. **B**, Within-recording split-half coefficients for auditory 40-Hz SNR in ds005048 and frequency-specific ITPC in ds007648 and ds006780. The ds005048 estimate compares the first and second halves of the six common blocks; the ds007648 and ds006780 estimates are odd–even correlations corrected with the Spearman–Brown formula. **C**, Age- and sex-adjusted differences in closed-eye 10-Hz selectivity in ds006036. Relative to cognitively normal participants, selectivity was lower by 6.610 units in Alzheimer disease (95% CI −11.876 to −1.345) and by 7.174 units in frontotemporal dementia (95% CI −13.715 to −0.632; N=76). **D**, Age coefficients for the ds006780 40-Hz local log-SNR and ITPC responses, expressed as response SD per 1-SD increase in age. The standardized estimates were 0.405 (95% CI 0.198 to 0.612) and 0.417 (95% CI 0.233 to 0.600), respectively (N=111). Split-half coefficients do not estimate between-day test–retest reliability, and population sensitivity does not establish cognitive validity or treatment efficacy.

## Figure 4

**Figure 4. Diagnostic-group sensitivity and individual clinical validity diverge.** **A**, Participant-level associations between six resting-EEG spectral features and MMSE in ds004504 (N=88). Points are Spearman correlations and bars are participant-bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. MMSE was fixed at 30 in the control group, so these coefficients are not interpreted as an unqualified linear disease continuum. **B**, Adjusted Alzheimer disease–control and frontotemporal dementia–control contrasts in closed-eye 10-Hz selectivity in ds006036. These are same-data computational reproductions, not an external replication. **C**, Within-diagnosis associations between the closed-eye 10-Hz driving index and MMSE. The estimates were ρ=0.125 in Alzheimer disease (95% CI −0.233 to 0.442; n=32) and ρ=−0.237 in frontotemporal dementia (95% CI −0.611 to 0.221; n=21). **D**, Association between the auditory 40-Hz stimulation-minus-rest SNR and MMSE in ds005048 (ρ=0.085, 95% CI −0.283 to 0.417; n=33). Intervals crossing zero are retained as estimates with uncertainty and are not treated as proof of no association. A group contrast does not by itself establish reliable individual ordering or a continuous severity marker.

## Figure 5

**Figure 5. High within-recording reliability does not guarantee concurrent behavioral validity.** **A**, Cluster-robust within-participant associations of trial-level 36- and 40-Hz phase-projection scores with target accuracy in ds007648. The 36-Hz estimate was 0.115 log-odds per within-participant SD (95% CI 0.047 to 0.183; q=0.00386), whereas the 40-Hz interval crossed zero. **B**, Corresponding associations with log response time among correct trials; both intervals crossed zero. **C**, Full-length split-half reliability plotted against the accuracy coefficient for the same frequency-specific ITPC measure. Reliability was 0.870 at 36 Hz and 0.985 at 40 Hz, while the 40-Hz accuracy coefficient was weaker and imprecise. **D**, Adjusted associations of 40-Hz local log-SNR, Morlet power, and ITPC with 40-Hz d-prime in ds006780 (N=111); all 95% confidence intervals crossed zero. The ITPC comparison is matched in metric and participant-level aggregation, whereas reliability of aggregate ITPC is not transferred to trial-state phase scores or local log-SNR. The 36-Hz result is a concurrent association and does not show that experimentally increasing the response would improve accuracy.

## Figure 6

**Figure 6. In-sample cognitive associations do not establish transportable or incremental prediction.** **A**, Adjusted associations of ds006780 40-Hz local log-SNR, Morlet power, and ITPC with FSIQ in the technically usable release (n=109). All three estimates were negative; the local log-SNR coefficient was −5.13 FSIQ points per response SD (95% CI −9.49 to −0.78). **B**, Eligibility and specification analysis for the local log-SNR coefficient. The interval crossed zero after restriction to FSIQ >80 and after the combined age and FSIQ eligibility restriction. **C**, Held-out change in prediction error after neural features were added to the corresponding non-neural model. Negative values favor the neural model. Neural features did not clearly improve ds007648 accuracy or response-time prediction, increased ds006780 d-prime error, and did not clearly improve ds006780 FSIQ or personalized-theta benefit prediction. **D**, Claim-level status map for detectability, within-recording reliability, population sensitivity, concurrent validity, incremental prediction, and causal or clinical interpretation. Cell labels are qualitative evidence states rather than effect sizes, a validation score, or a meta-analysis. The FSIQ analysis is exploratory and ability-range dependent; it is not interpreted as a transportable cognitive biomarker.

## Figure 7

**Figure 7. Target engagement, acute task-performance advantage, neural–behavioral coupling, and individual-benefit prediction are separable claims.** Results are from the frozen raw-EEG cohort of 35 healthy older adults and compare theta-plus with non-rhythmic stimulation. **A**, Participant-level paired differences in frontocentral inter-trial phase coherence at the common analysis frequency. The mean difference was 0.088 (95% CI 0.051 to 0.124; paired d<sub>z</sub>=0.830; p=2.23×10<sup>−5</sup>). Evoked local log signal-to-noise ratio increased by 7.518 dB, whereas total- and induced-power contrasts crossed zero, localizing the effect to phase-consistent activity under the tested decomposition. **B**, Paired behavioral differences. Median correct response time was 27.727 ms lower (95% CI 19.284 to 36.171 ms lower), inverse efficiency was 25.856 ms lower (95% CI 16.257 to 35.455 ms lower), and the accuracy interval crossed zero. **C**, Condition-specific slopes of leave-one-trial-out phase alignment for correctness and correct-trial log response time. All four intervals crossed zero and all Holm-adjusted p values were 1.000. **D**, Nested leave-one-participant-out incremental prediction of inverse-efficiency benefit. Neural features improved mean absolute error by 3.874 ms relative to the training-fold mean (95% bootstrap CI −0.431 to 8.032) but worsened it by 1.024 ms relative to the baseline-feature model (95% CI −3.207 to 1.013); the prespecified prediction gate did not pass. The active-control contrast supports stimulus-locked target engagement and an acute task-performance advantage, but not condition-specific neural–behavioral coupling, mediation, a deployable treatment-selection rule, chronic efficacy, or disease modification.

## Canonical terminology and source alignment

- Frequency-following response or target engagement is used unless stronger entrainment criteria are tested.
- Reliability means within-recording split-half reliability unless another design is explicitly named.
- Concurrent association, held-out incremental prediction, and treatment efficacy remain distinct endpoints.
