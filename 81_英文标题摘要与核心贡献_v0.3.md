# Title

From target engagement to cognitive inference: testing four inferential transitions across seven human rhythmic-sensory EEG cohorts

# Abstract

Rhythmic sensory stimulation produces stimulus-locked electroencephalographic responses, but their cognitive and clinical meaning is uncertain. We conducted secondary analyses across seven public human EEG datasets. The synthesis tested four inferential transitions: detectable response to state-general mechanism, within-recording reliability to matched cognitive validity, mean active-control benefit to within-participant neural coupling, and in-sample association to held-out incremental prediction. Closed-eye 10 Hz visual selectivity differed by diagnosis but had low split-half reliability and imprecise within-diagnosis cognitive associations. In an older-adult auditory cohort, 32/35 participants showed a positive 40 Hz stimulation-minus-rest response and split-half stability was 0.838, whereas its Mini-Mental State Examination association was 0.085 (95% CI −0.283 to 0.417). A developmental analysis paired reliable 40 Hz inter-trial phase coherence (0.972) and d-prime (0.931) with an adjusted association of 0.058 outcome SD (95% CI −0.255 to 0.370). In a frozen 35-participant older-adult cohort, theta-plus versus non-rhythmic stimulation increased frontocentral inter-trial phase coherence by 0.088 (95% CI 0.051 to 0.124) and evoked local signal-to-noise ratio by 7.52 dB (95% CI 4.52 to 10.51), without total- or induced-power differences. Median correct response time decreased by 27.73 ms (95% CI −36.17 to −19.28) and inverse efficiency by 25.86 ms (95% CI −35.45 to −16.26), while the accuracy interval crossed zero. However, condition-specific phase-alignment–behavior slopes were unsupported, and neural features did not consistently improve nested held-out prediction. Thus, the active-control contrast supported target engagement and an acute task-performance advantage, but not a condition-specific neural–behavioral mechanism proxy. Across cohorts, target engagement, reliability, sensitivity, cognitive validity, coupling, and prediction did not substitute for one another. These results specify the validation tests required before rhythmic-sensory EEG responses can support mechanistic, cognitive-biomarker, treatment-selection, or surrogate-endpoint claims.

# Keywords

rhythmic sensory stimulation; electroencephalography; frequency-following response; target engagement; cognitive validity; reliability; biomarker validation; out-of-sample prediction

# Core contribution statement

The contribution is an empirical cross-cohort test of four inferential transitions, not a new name for a validation hierarchy. Each transition is tied to a distinct estimand and a result that can support, constrain, or falsify the proposed upgrade. The study therefore evaluates where evidence obtained from rhythmic-sensory EEG stops, while preserving positive antecedent findings.
